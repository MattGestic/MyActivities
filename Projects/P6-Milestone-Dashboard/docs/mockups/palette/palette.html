Schedule Reporting and Evaluation Tool · palette proposal for approval

A calmer, business-grade palette that keeps your grey page and blue header

What stays: the light cool-grey page and the navy header, refined a touch deeper. What changes: the bright lavender accent is retired. Selection, focus and "now" markers move to one confident accent, with two options below. A greyer slate, related to the navy, marks the annotation workspace. Status colours are re-tuned so they stay distinct on white and on dark, and each carries its contrast rating.

1. Foundation: neutrals and brand

These carry 90% of the screen. Hex values are light theme, then dark theme.

2. Accent: choose one

The accent is used sparingly: selected chips, focus rings, the current week, #ID chips and the edited mark. Both options work on white and on the dark surface.

Option A · Petrol teal Recommended

Fresh without being loud. It sits next to navy the way a firm's secondary colour does, and it stays clearly separate from the status blue and green.

Option B · Brass

Warmer and more "annual report". Classy on navy, but it sits close to the amber "at risk" status, so risk has to lean redder to stay distinct.

3. Status and health

Mapped to the state-and-health scheme under discussion: fill shows state, and the outline colour shows health. These are semantic colours and stay separate from the accent.

3b. Milestone groups and month headers For approval

Group bands are ordered, not meaningful, so they use muted tones from the navy, slate and teal family. They carry no green, amber or red, so a group can never be read as a status. Month headers alternate two navy shades, where today each season has its own colour. Every tone takes white text at AA or better in both themes.

Tokens: --color-band-1..5, --color-month-a / -b, with the band divider derived from the on-band text colour at 20%. The month colour moves out of the schedule data (MONTHS[].bg) into CSS, so a theme change never needs a data change.
